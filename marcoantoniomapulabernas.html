Layout: Auto follows how you hold the phone. If your phone is locked to portrait, pick a Landscape option and turn the phone the way it says; the picture rotates for you. Low-end mode shrinks textures, hides clouds, night lights and stars, thins storms, caps frames at 24 fps and trims 3D city detail. Turn it on if the game stutters.
Tap a tool, then tap the map to place a storm. Use Look to select storms, then Steer to redirect a typhoon. Run evacuations and pumps from a city card. Tap posts to read replies and translate them. Pick a map design in Settings or under Climate. Open the AI tab in the feed to ask Eyewall AI for forecasts and risk. Climate → Radar has reflectivity, velocity, echo tops, rain rate, lightning and IR. Tap a city then open its 3D view for power lines, slums and flying roofs.

Physics notes. This is a bulk game model, not numerical weather prediction. Typhoon intensity relaxes toward a potential set by sea temperature, latitude, shear and dry air, with rapid intensification only when the core is organised, shear is low and the water is very warm. Organisation, eye clarity, asymmetry, eyewall replacement and wind radii are tracked per storm and shared by the clouds, radar, wind field and info panel. Supercells use an estimated CAPE, CIN and shear to decide growth, shape and decay. A coarse global grid (5 degree cells, 2 layers) carries air temperature, humidity, cloud water and wind: thermal pressure anomalies drive wind with Coriolis and friction, convergence and mountains give lift, lift condenses vapor with latent heating, and cloud water turns into rain. Storms add ascent, moisture and cold pools to it, and it supplies their environmental shear and dry air. Satellite and radar water-vapor, visible, infrared, rain and pressure products are drawn from that grid plus the storm cloud field. It is far coarser than a real forecast model.
